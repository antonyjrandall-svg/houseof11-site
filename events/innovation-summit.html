Once a year, the whole house comes home with a purpose: to put the world's ocean-energy technology, the capital that funds it, the industries that will buy its power and the Government of Barbados in one room — until the island that made the rum is powered by its own sea.

Why this one exists

The homecoming with a purpose

11 was founded on a promise: every glass goes home. The Innovation Summit is where the promise does its heaviest lifting — the one event each year held on the island itself, where the club's network goes to work for Barbados. The ten voyages before it build the crew; the eleventh puts the crew to use.

Three working days

Day I

The Island

Barbados states its demand: the energy picture, the pilot sites, the policy runway. The technology companies answer with what they can deliver, and when. Working tables — no stage, no slides read aloud.

Day II

The Deals

Offtake conversations, pilot structures, finance tables pairing funds with projects. The day ends with letters of intent drafted in the room. The summit's currency is signatures, not applause.

Day III

The Proof

The coast, the proposed sites, the distillery plan, the university's work — closed with a Barbados evening only a rum house can host. The room leaves as advocates.

The room we are building

Who sits at the table

  • Ocean energyThe wave and ocean-thermal companies already working toward island-scale power — including those with existing Barbados agreements.
  • Government & islandThe island's development and energy institutions, ministries and utility — the hosts of the demand.
  • CapitalFounders Club members, climate and infrastructure funds, development finance — the people who can say yes in the room.
  • Science & demandThe university's island-energy research, and the industrial offtakers — our distillery first among them — whose commitments make pilots bankable.

The roster is in formation. No participant is announced until confirmed — names are spoken to members, never printed.

What success means, in writing

The island keeps the score.

Anchor-customer letters of intent between island offtakers and ocean-energy developers · a standing compact that reconvenes every November, progress reviewed in person · a funding table matched to the pilot pipeline · and the story, told on every bottle and in every bar we reach: the island that taught the world to turn cane into gold, learning to turn waves into power.
