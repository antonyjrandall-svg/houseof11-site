What the harbour is

Alongside the hundred. Never inside it.

The Founders 100 are individuals — one hundred seats across two classes, named once, original forever. Their table does not move. The harbour is different water: a small number of berths for institutions, family offices and strategic partners who sail with the house at scale and for the long term. When the berths are taken, the harbour is full. It does not expand.

What a berth carries

I

First water

Berthed ahead of the wider round the house has already charted — the believer’s position, taken early, on paper agreed in person.

II

The long voyage

Standing rights to stay aboard as the house grows — the harbour is built for partners who intend to be here at the end of the story, not the first chapter.

III

The still, first

First sight of the house’s next vessel — the distillery — before its water opens to anyone else.

IV

The club’s table

Each berth may name two principals, invited into the Founders Club as members — a standing extended, never a bundle: the club is a club, and the berth is the berth.

Every figure, document and term lives behind the harbour’s own gate — nothing commercial is written on this page, by design. The house’s full structure & tax atlas opens by name: request the conversation below, and it arrives watermarked with yours.

How a ship comes in

Five steps, all of them human.

  1. The conversation. You and the founders, person to person. The sorting question is asked here, never by a form.
  2. The checks. Institutional verification — who you are, how you hold, that you qualify.
  3. The room. The house’s data room opens to you by name: verified entry, agreement signed at the door, every page watermarked.
  4. The paper. Terms agreed in person, signed on counsel’s paper. Nothing about the harbour is standard, deliberately.
  5. The berth. Your flag in the harbour, your principals at the club’s table, and the island’s November room every year after.
