Our vision for 11

Raise a glass.
Raise an island.

For three hundred years, rum built the world. Ships, cities, fortunes, empires — all of it raised on the cane of one small island. Almost none of it ever flowed back.

The idea

Our vision for 11

11 was born from a simple, uncomfortable idea: the world is full of industries that desperately need their money to flow back to the roots and heritage that built them. Rum is the oldest case on earth. So we started there — with rum, and with the island that invented it.

Our vision is a full loop. The way we serve, the way we make, and the money we earn — all of it built to flow home. The world's most exciting bars pouring it. A bartender academy training the island's own. A distillery designed to run on the island's own sea — powered by the ocean, aiming to be the most sustainable still on earth. And a company structured so that as 11 grows, investment, jobs and innovation land back in Barbados.

This is bigger than a drinks brand. The island that taught the world to make rum is now teaching the world something again — how an island powers itself, feeds itself, funds itself. Barbados has set out to be fossil-fuel-free by 2030, and to redraw how the world's money reaches its islands. 11 is a company built the way the island is asking the world's money to behave. Every bottle is a small act of that future.

The team

Four lifetimes went into this.

Between us: a lifetime building some of the most exciting bars and restaurants in the world, and the extraordinary liquids poured in them. The rare gift of creating rums no one else can — the liquids behind some of the most iconic musicians and creators alive. A whole life spent distilling on the island where rum was born. And thirty years in culture, the last twenty at the intersection of sustainability and innovation.

We put all of it into one company, with one goal: to build the most innovative rum company ever made — one that makes enough money to give it back to the roots and heritage of the very island that gave rum to the world.

We don't print our names here. Pour the rum, follow the story, find the characters.

It takes a world to raise an island. It takes an island to save the world. We're starting with the one that taught it to make rum.

Barbados is the island of eleven parishes that taught the world to turn cane into gold. 11 is named for it — and built to repay it. Every glass goes home.

The three turns of the loop

Serve

Rum, served like nowhere else

11 will stand at the forefront of how rum is served — in the world's great bars, through our bartender academy in Barbados, and through the Adventurers' Club, doing things on the progressive side of change that no one has done before.

Make

The distillery that runs on the sea

Barbados is already moving: a national wave-energy pilot has been signed for the island, and a university-born venture is turning distillery wastewater and sargassum seaweed into clean fuel. 11's distillery is being designed to plug into that future — powered and watered by the ocean, its by-products feeding the island's energy, aiming to be the most sustainable distillery in the world.

Give

Capital that goes home

The company is structured so that as 11 grows, investment, jobs and innovation flow back into Barbados — the academy, the distillery, the projects in our wake — until the island that made the rum becomes the most exciting place in the world.

From one of the founders

Building in reverse.

For five hundred years, the story of business has sailed one direction. Find the island. Take the treasure. Sail away. The ships changed — galleons became companies, cannons became contracts — but the direction never did. I spent thirty years in culture watching that same voyage leave port over and over: culture used to extract, economies mined until they were hollow, wealth trickling upward while everyone was told it was coming down.

I started 11 to sail the other way.

We are not here to extract from the world. We are here to give back. We are here to do the opposite of what everybody else has done — and I’ll be honest with you, the opposite is harder. There is no chart for sailing backwards. It is confusing, because we are not navigating by the usual stars: more houses, more cars, more for us. We threw that compass overboard. Ours points home.

This isn’t about getting richer for ourselves. It’s about getting richer for everyone. It isn’t about using culture to extract — it’s about using culture to build culture. It isn’t about extracting economies — it’s about helping communities grow themselves. The same system of economics the old ships were built on — we haven’t rejected it. We’ve reverse-engineered it. Same winds, same water, same money. We just turned the ship around, so the treasure flows back to the island instead of away from it.

Will it work? It’s harder, so maybe. That’s the honest answer. But I know what I’m sailing for: a day when our future generations look back at us and say — those ones actually did something. They took the machine that was built to take, and they made it give.

Raise a glass. Raise an island. Every glass goes home.

Antony Randall
One of the founders · The House of 11
